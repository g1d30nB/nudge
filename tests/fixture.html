Plans your whole team will actually read

A planning tool for teams that ship every week, built around the habit of asking what the numbers mean before asking what the chart says. It makes the last ten per cent of a release plan less tedious to finish.

Case study

A platform programme, 2019 to 2024.

We inherited an app with a two-star rating and a backlog of nineteen markets, each with its own idea of what the app was for.

The first year was spent on the unglamorous parts: a design system that could survive translation, a research cadence that reached actual users, and a product council that met weekly.

By the third year the rating was above four in every market, and the app had become the default channel for connected services.

Billing, scheduled reports and account settings each shipped as a separate squad's work, then were folded into one journey once the research showed customers did not think in features.

The design system moved from a Sketch library to coded components in the second year, which halved the time from a design review to a release candidate.

Nineteen markets became one release train with market flags, and the localisation backlog went from months to days.

Our largest programme ran across four years and eleven teams, and set the pattern for every connected product that followed.
